NOM standards: how to check whether they apply to your product

Updated: 4 October 2026

What Normas Oficiales Mexicanas are, when they apply and how to work out which requirements apply to a specific product.

With every new product, the first question is the same: is it covered by a NOM? NOM stands for Norma Oficial Mexicana. These are mandatory technical regulations issued by Mexican ministries, each within its own remit. They cover product safety, technical specifications or what must appear on the label and in the instructions. Alongside them are NMX standards, which are voluntary. When someone in Mexico talks about a standard you have to meet, they almost always mean a NOM.

The starting point for finding out which standards apply is the tariff code. The Ministry of Economy publishes Annex 2.4.1 to its foreign trade rules: a list of tariff codes for which customs checks compliance with a specific standard at clearance. If your code is on the list, the goods won't get through without proof of compliance.

If your code isn't on the list, that doesn't mean the standard doesn't apply. Many standards cover products sold in Mexico, not only imported ones. In that case enforcement comes later, in the market, carried out by PROFECO, the consumer protection agency, among others. Standards also have their own scope and exemptions, so the annex should always be read together with the text of the standard.

What compliance looks like in practice varies a lot. For some products a correct Spanish label is enough. For others, a verification unit issues a document for each model, and its number is entered in the customs declaration. For others still, you need a certificate from an accredited certification body, issued after testing in a Mexican laboratory. European certificates and test reports help prepare the file, but they don't replace the Mexican procedure.

Since 1 January 2026, getting this wrong has become much more expensive. If the required compliance with a standard can't be shown at import, the fine can be 250 to 300% of the commercial value of the goods. That's why we establish the applicable standards right at the start, together with the tariff code.

Legal basis

  • Ley de Infraestructura de la Calidad, arts. 154–156
  • Annex 2.4.1 to the Ministry of Economy's general rules on foreign trade (Anexo 2.4.1)
  • Ley Aduanera, art. 178(IV), as in force from 1 January 2026 (DOF 19.11.2025)

The articles are for information only and do not replace an analysis of a specific product. Requirements can depend on the type of product, its classification and how it is sold.
