Glossary: trade and import in Mexico

Updated: 4 October 2026

Below are the acronyms and terms that come up in any conversation about importing into Mexico. Some have no exact equivalent in Europe, so we keep the original names and explain briefly what they mean.

ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ

A

Agente aduanal
Licensed customs broker. Only a broker can clear goods through customs on the importer's behalf, under a licence (patente) issued by the Mexican authorities.
Anexo 2.4.1
Annex to the Ministry of Economy's foreign trade rules listing the tariff codes for which compliance with a NOM is checked at clearance.

C

CFDI
Mexican electronic invoice. A company that can't issue one can't legally sell in Mexico.
COFEPRIS
Federal Commission for Protection against Health Risks, part of the Ministry of Health. It oversees food, medicines and cosmetics, among other things.
Comercializadora
A company that imports goods in its own name on behalf of another company and resells them.
Constancia de conformidad
A document from an inspection or verification unit confirming that a label meets the standard.
Contraseña oficial NOM
The official mark showing compliance with a standard, placed on certified products.

D

DTA
Customs processing fee charged on the customs declaration.

E

EMA
Mexican Accreditation Entity. It accredits laboratories and certification, inspection and verification bodies.
EUR.1
Movement certificate proving the EU origin of goods, issued by the customs authority of the exporting country.

F

Fracción arancelaria
The eight-digit code under which goods are classified in the Mexican tariff.

I

IEPS
Special tax on production and services, applied to certain groups of products.
IGI
General import duty. The rate depends on the tariff code and the origin of the goods.
IVA
Mexican value added tax, the equivalent of VAT. The standard rate is 16%.

L

LIGIE
The law on general import and export duties, which contains the Mexican customs tariff.

N

NICO
Two-digit extension to the tariff code. Together with the fracción it makes up the ten-digit code used in the customs declaration.
NMX
A voluntary Mexican standard, as opposed to a mandatory NOM.

P

Padrón de Importadores
The SAT's register of importers. As a rule you can't import without being registered, although there are exceptions.
Padrón de Sectores Específicos
An additional importers' register required for certain groups of goods.
Pedimento
The Mexican customs declaration.
Precio estimado
A reference price set for certain goods. Declaring a lower value triggers a customs valuation review.
PROFECO
Federal Consumer Protection Agency. Among other things, it checks that products on the market comply with standards.

R

RFC
Mexican tax identification number.

S

SAT
Mexico's tax administration, which is also responsible for customs.
SENASICA
National Service for Agri-Food Health, Safety and Quality, part of the Ministry of Agriculture.

T

TLCUEM
The EU–Mexico free trade agreement, in force since 2000.

U

UMA
The unit used to calculate fines and fees in Mexico. In 2026 it is worth 117.31 pesos.
Unidad de inspección / Unidad de verificación
An accredited inspection or verification unit that checks compliance, for example of labels, and issues documents such as a constancia or a dictamen.

V

VUCEM
Mexico's single window for foreign trade, the electronic system used to file clearance documents.
