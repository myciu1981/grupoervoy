Importer of record in Mexico: who can do it

Updated: 4 October 2026

Who can be the importer, what role the importer plays in the process and what options you have if your company doesn't yet have its own structure in Mexico.

As a rule, only a party with a Mexican RFC tax number, registered in the SAT importers' register (Padrón de Importadores), can appear as importer on the Mexican customs declaration, the pedimento. A European company without a Mexican registration therefore can't clear its own goods, even if it pays the freight and sells directly to a Mexican customer.

Some tariff codes also require registration in a sector-specific register. That registration belongs to the importer, not the manufacturer, and it can't be borrowed for a single shipment. It's worth checking this before you start talking to a potential importer.

In practice, the importer can be:

  • a distributor who buys the goods from you and imports them in their own name,
  • an import service company, known in Mexico as a comercializadora, which imports goods in its own name and resells them,
  • your own company in Mexico, once registered with the SAT.

Import service companies usually charge a percentage of the invoice value, sometimes plus a fixed fee per shipment. On small shipments that fixed fee can outweigh the percentage, so compare the whole cost structure, not just the rate.

The importer is answerable to customs for what it declares. It pays duties and taxes and bears the consequences of errors in the code, the documents or the label. Since 2026, importing without showing the required compliance with a standard can lead to a fine of 250 to 300% of the goods' value, so a good importer will ask a lot of questions about the paperwork. That's a good sign.

When choosing, check whether the importer can pay in euros, ask for its current tax status certificate (constancia de situación fiscal) and agree in writing whose name the certificates and compliance documents will be issued in. If you ever change partner, that's what decides the cost of the switch.

If you don't yet have a company in Mexico, someone needs to represent you on the ground and make sure the importer, the customs broker and the certification bodies work to the same timeline. That doesn't have to be the importer; often it's better to keep those roles separate.
