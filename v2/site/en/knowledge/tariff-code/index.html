Your product's tariff code: why it matters so much

Updated: 4 October 2026

The tariff code affects duties, regulatory requirements and import paperwork. How it's determined and when it's worth getting an official ruling.

A different tariff code can decide whether goods enter Mexico duty-free or pay the full rate. The tariff code is the number under which goods appear in the customs tariff. Mexico uses ten-digit codes: eight digits form the fracción arancelaria and the last two are the NICO, a further subdivision for statistical purposes. The first six digits come from the international Harmonized System and are shared by Mexico and the EU. After that the systems diverge.

One code determines several things at once: the duty rate, the standards checked at clearance, any sector-specific registration the importer needs and, for some goods, a reference price. Declaring a value below that price automatically sends the entry for review.

Sometimes it's only a careful look at what the product is made of that changes its classification. With a new code, a standard may stop applying, the importer's sector registration may no longer be needed and the rule of origin may change. One number drags half the requirements along with it.

It also happens that two customs authorities are both right and still disagree. The Harmonized System is binding in English and French, and each country uses its own translation. If a single word is lost in translation, European and Mexican customs can each, under their own law, put the same goods under different headings. This usually comes to light when the proof of origin is being issued and the goods are already on their way. That's why we agree the code in writing before shipment with the manufacturer, the European customs agent, the importer and the customs broker in Mexico.

If the classification is in doubt, you can ask the Mexican customs authority for a binding ruling before importing. The legal basis is article 47 of the Ley Aduanera. Importers, exporters and customs brokers can apply. The procedure is free and the authority has three months to reply. In the meantime the goods can be cleared by paying the highest of the disputed duty rates. The ruling binds the authority with respect to the applicant and is strong evidence of due diligence.

Legal basis

  • Ley Aduanera, art. 47
  • Código Fiscal de la Federación (Federal Tax Code), arts. 34 and 37
  • Ley de los Impuestos Generales de Importación y de Exportación (LIGIE)

The articles are for information only and do not replace an analysis of a specific product. Requirements can depend on the type of product, its classification and how it is sold.
