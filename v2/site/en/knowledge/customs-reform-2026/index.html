Mexico's 2026 customs reform: what changed

Updated: 4 October 2026

The main changes to Mexican customs law in 2026 and what they mean for companies importing into Mexico.

In short

A broad reform of the Ley Aduanera has applied since 1 January 2026. The fine for importing without showing compliance with non-tariff requirements, including NOM standards, rose from 70–100% to 250–300% of the goods' commercial value, and customs brokers now carry more liability and ask for more documents. It's worth checking the tariff code, the label and the proof of origin before shipping.

On 19 November 2025, Mexico's official gazette published a decree amending the Customs Law, the Ley Aduanera. It amends, adds or repeals more than a hundred articles, and most of the changes have applied since 1 January 2026. Here are the ones that really affect a European manufacturer and its importer.

The biggest change concerns fines. For importing without showing the required compliance with non-tariff requirements, including NOM standards, the fine until the end of 2025 was 70 to 100% of the goods' commercial value, and commercial information standards were excluded. Since 1 January 2026 it has been 250 to 300%, and the exclusion is gone. A labelling error that used to be treated more leniently can now cost two and a half to three times the value of the goods themselves. Fines for incorrect data in the value declaration and for late submission of documents have also gone up.

The reform increased customs brokers' liability. A broker must check documents and compliance thoroughly, without the exemptions from liability that existed before. In practice, brokers ask for more documents and ask more questions. The first clearance takes longer, but the importer is better protected.

Importers must keep an automated inventory control system and an electronic file for each operation, with invoices, contracts, valuation documents and proof of payment. Data in the customs declaration can only be corrected before the automated selection for inspection is triggered. On the other hand, a new article allows preferential tariff treatment to be claimed after clearance, within the time limits of the relevant trade agreement.

A separate change is the decree of 29 December 2025, which raised duties on 1,477 tariff codes to between 5% and 50%. It applies to countries Mexico has no trade agreement with, above all China. EU goods with proof of origin keep their preferential rates, so the gap between a shipment with valid proof of origin and one without it is now much wider.

The takeaway is simple: check the tariff code, the label and the proof of origin before shipping, because fixing them at the border now costs far more than it did a year ago.

Legal basis

  • Decreto por el que se reforman, adicionan y derogan diversas disposiciones de la Ley Aduanera (DOF 19.11.2025)
  • Ley Aduanera, arts. 59, 89, 89 Bis, 178, 184-B and 185, as in force from 1 January 2026
  • Decree of 29.12.2025 (DOF) amending the LIGIE tariff
