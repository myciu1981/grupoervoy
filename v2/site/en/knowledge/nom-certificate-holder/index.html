Who can hold a NOM certificate

Updated: 4 October 2026

What options a manufacturer outside Mexico has and why the choice of certificate holder matters for importing and selling later.

You often hear in Mexico that only a Mexican company can hold a NOM certificate, so a foreign manufacturer has to get it in its importer's name. That isn't always true. Some standards allow the certificate holder to be a manufacturer from another country, provided it has legal representation in Mexico.

This matters more than it first seems. Whoever holds the certificate decides who can legally sell the product under it. The certificate can be extended to a designated company, such as a distributor, but it can't be transferred to another party. If your distributor holds the certificate and the relationship ends, the certificate stays with them and your new partner starts from scratch. If you or your representative in Mexico hold it, you can change distributors without repeating the whole process.

Holding a certificate also brings obligations. The holder is answerable to the Mexican authorities for the product's compliance, pays for surveillance and renewals, and the certificate is valid for a limited time, usually one to three years. During that time the certification body can take samples for follow-up testing.

The rules differ from standard to standard. Some products have no certificate at all, while for others the documents are issued in the importer's name. That's why we decide whose name the certificate will be in before the application is filed, together with the import model.

Legal basis

  • NOM-003-SCFI-2014, clauses 9.4.1, 9.5.3.5 and 9.5.5 (an example of a standard that allows a foreign certificate holder)
  • Ley de Infraestructura de la Calidad

The articles are for information only and do not replace an analysis of a specific product. Requirements can depend on the type of product, its classification and how it is sold.
