How to start selling your product in Mexico: first steps

Updated: 4 October 2026

Where to start if you're planning to sell in Mexico: the order of steps, the key decisions and where manufacturers most often lose time.

Most conversations with manufacturers begin with the question of customers: who will buy this, and at what price. It's a good question, just asked a little too early. Before you look for buyers, you need to know whether your product can legally enter Mexico, what it will cost once cleared, and who will import it. Those answers set the price, and the price shapes everything else.

The first step is the tariff code. It determines the duty rate, the standards the product must meet and whether the importer needs additional registrations. The code has to be agreed on both sides of the Atlantic before the goods leave the factory, because European and Mexican customs can classify the same product differently.

The second step is standards. Some products must comply with Mexican NOM standards. Sometimes that just means a correct Spanish label; sometimes it means a certificate issued after testing in Mexico. The CE mark and EU certificates don't replace the Mexican procedure, although they help with the paperwork. This stage usually takes longest, and it sets the timeline for the whole project.

The third step is origin and duties. The EU–Mexico agreement allows many European products into Mexico duty-free, but only with valid proof of origin. There are exceptions too, so work out the duty for your tariff code before you quote a price to anyone.

The fourth step is the importer. A company without a Mexican tax registration can't clear its own goods through customs. Someone in Mexico has to do it in their own name: a distributor, an import service company or your own company. That choice decides who is responsible for the goods at the border and whose name goes on the documents.

Only at this point does it make sense to talk about sales channels. It's worth asking what quality certificates retail chains or large buyers in your industry expect from suppliers. Missing a single document can close off an entire channel, however good the product is.

Most time is lost by doing things in the wrong order. Typical examples: designing the label before checking the standard's requirements, changing the tariff code on one side without telling the other, or sending test samples in a version that can't be sold in Mexico. Each of these mistakes costs weeks, sometimes months.

Shipping is usually the least of your worries. A container from northern Europe to Veracruz or Altamira takes roughly three to five weeks.
