Food labelling in Mexico and NOM-051 warning seals

Updated: 4 October 2026

How the black warning seals on packaging work, what they mean for label design and when a certificate of label compliance is worth having.

Anyone who has been to a Mexican supermarket has seen the black octagons on packaging: EXCESO AZÚCARES, EXCESO CALORÍAS, EXCESO SODIO. These are warning seals required by NOM-051, the standard that governs the whole label of food and drinks sold in Mexico.

A seal appears when a product exceeds the limits for calories, sugars, saturated fat, trans fat or sodium. They're calculated per 100 grams or 100 millilitres, not per serving, so reducing the portion size changes nothing. Many products considered ordinary in Europe get two or three seals in Mexico.

A seal is more than a symbol on the pack. Products carrying a seal can't feature characters or elements aimed at children, and health claims and some nutrition claims are banned. Since March 2025 such products can't be sold in schools. These are decisions that affect packaging design, and sometimes the whole brand concept.

The label itself also differs from the European one, even if it looks similar at first glance. Mexico presents allergens differently, describes sugars differently and wants some information in a different place or format. A label translated word for word from the EU version usually won't comply.

That's why order matters: first we check which seals the product will get and what can't be shown on the pack, and only then is the artwork designed. Since 2026, importing without showing the required compliance with a standard can lead to a fine of 250 to 300% of the goods' value, so fixing a label at the border is very expensive.

Do you need a constancia de conformidad?

A constancia de conformidad is a document in which an accredited unit confirms that a label meets the standard. For food labels it's voluntary: NOM-051 states that conformity assessment is not subject to certification, and the Ministry of Economy confirmed in 2020 that goods labelled before shipment can be imported without it.

Even so, it's often worth having for the first shipments, because it costs little compared with a possible fine. The manufacturer or a trading company can also apply, so you don't need an importer yet. The unit checks the label visually, making sure all the required information is there in the right form. It doesn't test the product in a laboratory, so responsibility for the values on the label stays with whoever prepared them.

Under some other standards, the compliance document isn't voluntary. It is issued per model and its number must be entered in the customs declaration.
